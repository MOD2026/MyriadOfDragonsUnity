# Myriad of Dragons — Player Recruitment War Plan

**Date:** 14 September 2026  
**Owner:** MT / Marketing  
**Mission:** Recruit the first 100 qualified followers/tester prospects through an aggressive,
human-led 14-day sprint.  
**Boundary:** Marketing only. No game-development files, production accounts, fabricated players,
or unapproved feature/release claims.

## The problem

The 13 September live audit found 26 sent posts produced one follower, zero reactions, zero
comments, and 0% recorded engagement. Most posts showed concept art or old animation, the queue was
nearly empty, and the visible posts did not carry a tracked conversion link.

The failure is not insufficient posting volume. The failure is that publishing has not been tied to
a repeatable recruitment motion.

Effective immediately:

- Stop treating views as recruitment.
- Stop leading with archive art.
- Stop opening new directories.
- Stop sending people to multiple destinations.
- Put current gameplay proof, a specific question, and one CTA at the center of every campaign.
- Spend at least as much daily effort on direct conversations and replies as on content production.

## One offer, one audience, one destination

### Offer

> Join the first 100 players following the rebuild of a dark-fantasy card battler built around
> committed formations and simultaneous clashes. Help decide what is readable, exciting, and worth
> improving next.

This is an invitation to follow and give feedback—not a promise of immediate access, a finished
build, a launch date, or an in-game reward.

### Primary audience

Target people who already show interest in:

1. digital card battlers and deckbuilders;
2. tactical formation games and autobattlers;
3. dark-fantasy indie games;
4. mobile strategy prototypes;
5. game-development playtests and feedback communities.

### Destination hierarchy

1. Public Kickstarter pre-launch **Notify me on launch** page, once verified active.
2. Until then, the public Itch.io project page.
3. Discord is the follow-up community destination, not a competing cold-traffic CTA.

Never send cold traffic to the Kickstarter `/build` editor URL. Use one public destination per
campaign window and preserve platform/content UTMs.

## The 14-day operating cadence

This is a recruitment sprint, not a passive content calendar.

### Daily non-negotiables

| Workstream | Daily quota | Standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Current-proof content | 1 core short or annotated gameplay post | First frame states the conflict; current footage appears immediately; one question; one CTA. |
| Platform adaptations | 3 | TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube/X receive native openings, not identical captions. |
| High-fit outreach | 15 contacts | Personalized to a creator, community organizer, curator, or active genre participant. Ask for one reaction, not promotion. |
| Community contribution | 10 useful replies | No project link unless relevant and permitted. Build recognition before asking. |
| Follow-ups | 5 | One polite follow-up after 3–4 days; no response after that means stop. |
| Response speed | Same day | Reply to every substantive comment or message, ideally within two hours while awake. |
| Measurement | 1 daily update | Record source, asset, hook, reach, engaged views, profile visits, clicks, follows/signups, replies, and next action. |

Fourteen-day output target: 14 proof posts, 210 personalized first contacts, 70 follow-ups, 140 useful
community contributions, two substantial devlogs, and two permitted community feedback threads.
These are activity commitments, not fabricated outcome guarantees.

## Daily battle rhythm

### Days 1–2 — repair the funnel

- Verify the one public CTA and test every UTM in a private browser.
- Rewrite every active bio to the same first-100 offer and destination.
- Replace vague “support my project” language with the first-100 feedback invitation.
- Build a prospect sheet of 100 micro-creators and community contributors. Prioritize audience fit
  over follower count.
- Cut three 8–20 second vertical clips from current gameplay: formation choice, simultaneous clash,
  and Avatar intervention where genuinely visible.

### Days 3–5 — launch three hooks

Publish one hook per day against the same proof:

1. **Decision:** “You commit the formation before the clash. What would you change?”
2. **Clarity challenge:** “Can you tell which side wins before the result appears?”
3. **Founder mission:** “I’m recruiting the first 100 players to challenge this rebuild.”

Send 15 tailored outreach messages daily. Each message names one specific video/post by the
recipient and asks one answerable question about the clip. Do not ask for coverage in the first
message.

### Days 6–7 — convert attention into conversations

- Turn the best comment into a response video or annotated post.
- Publish one honest “what viewers misunderstood” breakdown.
- Invite responders who gave useful feedback to follow the public page or join Discord.
- Run the first hard review: rank hooks by engaged viewing, profile visits, tracked clicks, and
  qualified replies. Archive the weakest hook.

### Days 8–10 — creator and community surge

- Contact the next 45 high-fit prospects using the winning hook.
- Offer micro-creators a private feedback preview only if an approved preview asset exists.
- Publish one permitted feedback thread in a relevant community; disclose the developer
  relationship and comply with that community's rules.
- Ask existing followers for one introduction to a card-battler or tactical-game player. No prizes,
  fake scarcity, or promised game benefits.
- Create two response posts from real questions instead of inventing FAQ demand.

### Days 11–12 — recruitment event

- Hold a 30-minute live or recorded founder teardown of one battle clip if the channel supports it.
- Use a single prompt: “Where did the battle stop making sense?”
- Invite attendees to the first-100 list after the value has been delivered.
- Personally follow up with every substantive participant within 24 hours.

If a live session cannot be supported cleanly, publish the teardown as a short series and keep the
same response workflow.

### Days 13–14 — concentrate and scale

- Recut the winning opening around the strongest retained moment.
- Publish a transparent sprint recap: what was posted, what players said, and what will change in
  the marketing message.
- Keep only the two channels and two hooks producing qualified actions.
- Build the next 14-day queue from winners; reduce every zero-signal channel to maintenance.

## Content doctrine

Use this mix during the sprint:

- **70% current gameplay proof** — decisions, clashes, readable outcomes, real interface moments.
- **20% founder/problem diary** — a concrete failure, question, or lesson.
- **10% archive/history** — only as clearly labelled context.

Every short should follow this structure:

1. **0–2 seconds:** visible tension or question.
2. **2–8 seconds:** show the decision, not a logo.
3. **8–15 seconds:** show the consequence or unresolved question.
4. **Final beat:** one CTA—join/follow the first 100.

YouTube states that Shorts distribution responds to whether viewers choose to watch, how long they
stay, and whether they are satisfied; raw starts alone are not enough. TikTok's Creator Search
Insights can identify searched topics with relatively little content, so use it daily to source
search-led hooks such as formation strategy, deckbuilder combat, tactical card games, and indie game
playtests.

## Outreach scripts

### Micro-creator

> Hi [Name]—your [specific video/post] made me think you would have a sharp read on this. I’m
> rebuilding a dark-fantasy formation card battler, and I’m testing one 15-second combat clip. No
> coverage ask: can you tell me where the decision becomes unclear? [link]

If they respond positively:

> That is useful—thank you. I’m assembling the first 100 people following the rebuild and feeding
> back on these decisions. If you want the next clip when it is ready, the public follow page is
> here: [tracked link]

### Genre player

> Quick tactical question: if both sides commit formations before a simultaneous clash, what one
> piece of information must be visible for the result to feel fair? I’m testing that problem in an
> indie card battler rebuild.

### Community thread opener

> Developer disclosure: I’m rebuilding this project and want criticism, not votes. Watch the first
> clash once. What did you expect to happen, and what actually happened?

Do not mass-DM, automate replies, repeat the same post across communities, or continue after someone
declines. Reddit explicitly treats repetitive exposure-seeking posts and large volumes of
unsolicited messages as spam; community moderators remain the authority.

## Recruitment scoreboard

Track the funnel by source every day:

`Source -> content view -> engaged view -> profile visit -> tracked click -> follow/signup -> reply -> retained participant`

### Primary scoreboard

| Metric | 14-day objective |
|---|---:|
| Personalized contacts sent | 210 |
| Qualified replies | 30+ |
| Tracked destination clicks | 100+ |
| New public-page followers/waitlist signups | 25+ minimum viable; 50+ strong; 100 stretch |
| People giving actionable feedback twice | 10+ |

The 100-player target is a stretch outcome, not something to fake. The controllable goal is a full
pipeline and enough conversations to identify a repeatable acquisition source.

### Kill and scale rules

- A creative with two fair distributions and zero profile visits is retired.
- A channel with two consecutive seven-day periods and zero tracked clicks is paused.
- If people click but do not follow, stop increasing traffic and repair the destination/offer.
- If a small channel converts despite low reach, keep it and increase manual distribution.
- The top-performing hook gets three new cuts; the weakest hook gets no extra production time.
- Archive art cannot be the winner unless it also produces qualified clicks or replies.

## What “aggressive” means here

Aggressive means speed, focus, direct conversation, rapid creative iteration, and ruthless removal
of zero-signal work. It does not mean spam, fake users, purchased followers, undisclosed promotion,
or promises the project cannot keep.
